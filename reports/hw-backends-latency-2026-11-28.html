One HardwareInterface, six backends

The rack pendulum (CAN 11) behind one robot_msgs HardwareInterface · like_keras examples/hw_backends · 2026-11-28

One HardwareInterface reads and writes the pendulum on MuJoCo (nominal and twin of record), analytical, kinematic, real CAN 11 (live) and remote (same machine and LAN, through like_keras's AttachedBackend), all timed. The cloud leg is blocked on a Claude cloud sign-in or folder trust, not on the network: the tunnel it would use was measured end to end from here.

  • The interface itself costs ~0.02 ms (kinematic read). Everything above that is the backend: the real bus ~1.1–1.3 ms per CAN exchange, MuJoCo ~2.3 ms per 10 ms period (bam_rack_twin's 30 kHz moteus PID twin), the ODE ~0.04 ms.
  • Round trip = write a 2° step, then write/read until the joint has moved 0.02° toward it. On a sim that is one tick. On the real board it is ~5 bus ticks (~12 ms): the pendulum's inertia, not the link.
  • Remote adds ~1 ms on this host and over the LAN alike (p50): a stepped core checks for a command at 1 kHz. The LAN shows in the tail (p99 ~7 ms vs ~2 ms). Through Cloudflare's edge and back it is ~11 ms. A free-running core (real's default) answers on its own 100 Hz clock: a read waits up to one 10 ms sample, and a step shows two samples later (~20 ms).
  • "No friction" and "realistic" are one MuJoCo backend with two parameter sets (bam_rack_twin's twin of record, wrapped, not rewritten).
  • Found on the way: robot_msgs 4137538 (07:20 today, GroundTruth.meta: Any) makes ServoState un-encodable for like_keras's codec, so every attached core fails at start on robot_msgs main. The remote numbers here ran on robot_msgs 292b832.

Each read/write is one call through HardwareInterface (JointTable mapping included), over a write-then-read loop that holds position. Loop Hz = unpaced write+read ticks per second. A simulated backend advances one 10 ms period per write, so its "loop Hz" is how much faster than real time it can run.

Step responses

The first 2° step of each case. Sims on plant time; the real board on wall time. The frictionless MuJoCo and the analytical ODE are one curve (the ODE is dashed on top; the test suite holds them within 0.05°). The twin of record stops short in its LuGre friction band; the real board settles near 2°. Its PID in the deployment record is kp 200 / kd 4 N·m/rev (conf get 2026-10-29), while bam_ws memory says CAN 11 has run kp 400 / kd 8 since 11-15. Not checked live; if true, the twin runs stale gains.

Real run

Live, 2026-11-28T14:01:52Z: the CAN 11 slot was taken with rack_can11_want (deleted after), the rack's bus lock bus.272813EF.lock held for the run. The board was found at rest at 16.1° (its bottom) and every target was clamped to that ±3°; the steps were ±2°, the peak excursion 2.25°. The full arm was idle (arm-hold holding, no homing). 2000 write/read ticks, 100 steps, 0 faults.

Fingerprint refusal

The fingerprint is the robot's (its name and each joint with its servo key), not the parameter set: a client planning on the nominal sim may drive a core running the twin or the real board; it may not drive another robot.
